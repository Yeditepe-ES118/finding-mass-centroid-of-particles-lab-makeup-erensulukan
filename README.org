* Find the mass centroid of three particles
#+ATTR_HTML: :width 900px
[[./instructions.png]]

#+BEGIN_COMMENT
If three point masses are located at

\begin{equation}
\vec p_1 = \begin{bmatrix}
p_1^x\\
p_1^y
\end{bmatrix}\ , \ 
\vec p_2 = \begin{bmatrix}
p_2^x\\
p_2^y
\end{bmatrix}\ , \ 
\vec p_3 = \begin{bmatrix}
p_3^x\\
p_3^y
\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
and their corresponding masses are
\begin{equation}
m_1 \ , \ m_2 \ , \ m_3
\end{equation}
the position of the center of mass is
\begin{equation}
\vec c = \begin{bmatrix}
c_x\\
c_y
\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
where $c_x$ and $c_y$ is respectively,
\begin{equation}
c_x = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^3 p_i^x m_i}{\sum_{i=1}^3 m_i}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
c_y = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^3 p_i^y m_i}{\sum_{i=1}^3 m_i}
\end{equation}

Create a function called ~centroid~ in a new file called ~lab7.py~ so that it will take $p_1^x,p_1^y,p_2^x,p_2^y,p_3^x,p_3^y,m_1,m_2,m_3$ as inputs, and it will return the total mass, $c_x$ and $c_y$. It will also plot the particles and the center of mass.
#+END_COMMENT
